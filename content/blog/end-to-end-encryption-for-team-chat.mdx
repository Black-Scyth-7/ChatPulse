"End-to-end encrypted" gets printed on a lot of landing pages. It's worth
knowing what it actually promises — and what it doesn't — before you trust a
tool with your team's conversations.

## What end-to-end encryption means

Encryption in transit (TLS) protects your messages while they travel between
your device and a server. That's table stakes, and almost everyone has it. But
it means the server still sees your messages in plaintext when they arrive.

**End-to-end encryption (E2EE)** goes further: messages are encrypted on the
sender's device and can only be decrypted by the intended recipients. The
server relays ciphertext it cannot read. If our database were compromised
tomorrow, an attacker would find encrypted blobs, not your conversations.

The short version: with E2EE, the content of your messages is readable by your
team and no one else — not even us.

## Why team chat is harder than 1:1

Encrypting a conversation between two people is a well-understood problem.
Team chat breaks several of its assumptions:

- **Many participants.** A channel isn't two devices; it's dozens, each with
  their own keys, joining and leaving over time.
- **Membership changes.** When someone joins a channel, what can they read?
  When they leave, what should they lose access to going forward?
- **Multiple devices.** People read on a laptop, a phone, and a new laptop six
  months later. Keys have to move safely without ever exposing plaintext to
  the server.

The answer to "many participants who come and go" is a technique called a
**group ratchet** — the approach standardized in
[MLS (Messaging Layer Security)](https://messaginglayersecurity.rocks/). Each
channel maintains a shared group key that advances forward every time
membership changes, so a new member can't read history from before they
joined, and a departing member can't read messages sent after they left.

## The honest trade-offs

E2EE isn't free, and we'd rather tell you the costs than hide them:

- **Server-side search is limited.** We can't index what we can't read.
  ChatPulse does search on the client, over the messages your device has
  already decrypted.
- **Key loss is real.** If every device holding a channel's keys is lost, that
  history is gone. We mitigate this with encrypted key backup you control, but
  the math is unforgiving by design — that's the point.
- **Some integrations need to opt in.** A bot that summarizes a channel has to
  be an explicit member with its own keys, not a silent reader.
